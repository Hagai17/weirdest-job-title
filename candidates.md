# Candidates — Weirdest Job Title: Blind Judge Showdown

Prompt: one weird job title, a few words naming an odd job, inside a
1–60 character gate. Twenty candidates were drafted, every one
verified by script to sit inside the gate, and duelled pairwise on the
judge's criteria, in the judge's order: it must be a weird job title;
stranger than ordinary work — a job nobody has heard of or one with a
gloriously odd twist; funny, with a twist that lands on first read;
sounding like a real job rather than random word salad, specific
enough to picture the person doing it; and short enough to take in at
a glance.

## Winner (44 characters)

> Professional Mourner for Strangers' Funerals

## Runner-up (46 characters)

> Fortune Cookie Writer Who Only Writes Warnings

## Why the winner won

Both finalists name a job you can picture instantly. The runner-up's
twist is neat — a fortune cookie writer who deals only in warnings —
but it reads as an invented gag job: nobody hires for it. The winner
took the final on the second and fourth criteria. It is stranger: a
professional mourner is a genuinely paid profession almost nobody has
heard of, and the twist is carried by one word — *Strangers'* — which
turns an old occupation into an absurd one on first read: a person in
black, weeping on cue at the funeral of someone they never met, then
checking their watch for the next booking. It never stops sounding
like a real job, because it is one.

The rest of the slate fell in the earlier duels. The familiar real
jobs (Snake Milker, Chicken Sexer, Golf Ball Diver, Water Slide
Safety Tester, Professional Line Stander, Panda Nanny) lost on
strangeness — too many people have heard of them, so the twist never
lands. The most obscure (Worm Grunter, Iceberg Mover) lost on the
twist and the glance: nothing in the title tells you what is odd
about the work. The pure wordplay titles (Chief Apology Officer for
Delayed Trains, Director of Naps, Elevator Music Complaints Manager,
Human Statue Wrangler) lost on the real-job criterion — they sound
like joke titles, not work. Of the strong remainder, the Pet Psychic's
Appointment Scheduler lost because the scheduler is one step removed
from the weird job itself, and the Apology Gift Wrapper for Forgetful
Husbands fell to the runner-up on twist: the warnings-only fortune
writer is the sharper turn.
